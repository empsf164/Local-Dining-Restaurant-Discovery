The 7 Flakiest, Butteriest Croissants to Try This Week

A butter-lover's pursuit of golden honeycomb interiors and shatteringly crisp pastry layers across Central City's top French patisseries.

Claire Vance
Pastry Editor · Published October 3, 2026 · 5 min read

There is an unmistakable sound in the early morning at our city's finest bakeries: the delicate, crisp crackle of a freshly baked croissant giving way under fingertips. Inside, a warm, buttery steam escapes, carrying the caramelized aroma of cultured butter and slow-fermented dough.

1. The Golden Standard: Maison Crème Patisserie

Chef Julien Laurent doesn't take shortcuts. Using AOP Isigny Sainte-Mère cultured butter imported directly from Normandy, the lamination process at Maison Crème takes three full days of temperature-controlled folding and resting. The result is an open, translucent honeycomb crumb and an exterior that flakes like fine autumn leaves.

Maison Crème Patisserie

Editor's Morning Tip: Arrive between 8:00 AM and 9:00 AM when the morning batch hits the display counter still warm from the deck oven. Pair with their Ethiopian single-origin flat white.

2. The Nordic Twist: Fika & Botanica

While known for cardamom knots, Fika & Botanica introduces an inventive sourdough croissant fermented with wild elderflower yeast. The gentle acidity perfectly cuts through the rich butter profile, giving an unforgettable depth of flavor.

3. Twice-Baked Almond Elegance: Petite Tartine

If you prefer an indulgent afternoon treat, Petite Tartine takes day-one croissants, dips them in Madagascar vanilla syrup, fills them with velvety roasted almond frangipane, and bakes them a second time until golden with toasted slivered almonds.
